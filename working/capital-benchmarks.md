# Capital cost benchmarks — multilateral sources replace the press figure

The verdict previously leaned on a press report of Línea Z spend (~60 MXN
million/km). That figure is now **supporting context only**. A multilateral
primary source gives track renewal unit costs directly.

## Source

World Bank, *Serbia Railways Asset Management Plan Using Life-Cycle Costs*,
Report No. AUS0001540, May 2020 — **Table 11, Maintenance and Renewal Costs**.
`[wb-2020-serbia-railways-lcc]`

Retrieved via `documents1.worldbank.org`, which serves PDFs directly.
`ppiaf.org` and `ppp.worldbank.org` both return Cloudflare "Just a moment…"
challenges and were not circumvented.

## Against the supportable-capital ceiling

| Capture of all corridor articulated freight | Supportable MXN m/km |
|---|---|
| 100% (physically impossible; shown as a ceiling) | 13.6 – 18.6 |
| 50% | 6.8 – 9.3 |
| 30% | 4.1 – 5.6 |
| 15% | 2.0 – 2.8 |

- **General renewal (~12.2–14.3)** consumes essentially the **entire**
  supportable capital at a capture rate that cannot occur — leaving **nothing**
  for structures, signalling or right-of-way.
- **Partial renewal (~6.3–9.3)** clears only at ~50% capture.
- **At ≤30% capture, no World Bank renewal option clears at all.**

## What these figures exclude — and why it matters here

Table 11 is **track work only**: rail, sleepers, ballast, subsoil. It does not
include:

- **bridges, drainage and slope stabilisation** — the dominant uncertainty on
  this alignment, and the brief requires them carried as a separate line with
  their own range
- signalling and telecoms
- right-of-way clearance after 20+ years dormant
- any canyon / fault-zone terrain premium for the Cañada de Cuicatlán

So the true capital requirement sits **materially above** every figure in the
table. Every exclusion pushes the same direction: against the project.

## Triangulation of the light-rehabilitation benchmark

World Bank partial renewal (343,000–463,000/km) falls **inside** the UNESCAP
"< USD 500,000/route-km" figure the brief cites. Two independent multilateral
sources agree on the light end.

This matters procedurally: **`unescap.org` returned HTTP 403** and the brief's
UNESCAP figure could not be verified at source. It is now corroborated
independently rather than taken on trust.

## Transfer caveat, stated because it is real

Serbian unit costs do not transfer cleanly to Mexico — labour rates, material
costs, haul distances and contracting practice all differ, and the World Bank
report itself warns (p. 27) that tender prices understate outturn cost because
they omit scope variations, claims settlements and "below the line" costs. The
figures are used as an **order-of-magnitude international benchmark**, not as a
Mexican estimate. A Mexican unit cost remains unsourced.

Notably, the outturn-cost warning and the Mexican press precedent point the
same way: realised cost above benchmark, not below.
