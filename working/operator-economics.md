# Operator economics — FIT has never covered its operating costs

**Primary source.** ARTF *Anuario Estadístico Ferroviario* 2024, Tablas 7-3
(revenue) and 7-5 (operating cost), constant 2024 MXN, thousands. Not press.

This matters because **FIT (Ferrocarril del Istmo de Tehuantepec) is the
assignee of Vía Corta Oaxaca** — confirmed in ARTF Tabla 1-1. It is the entity
that would operate a reactivated line.

## FIT

| Year | Revenue | Operating cost | Result | Cost recovery |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2019 | 446,812 | 1,233,407 | −786,595 | **36.2%** |
| 2020 | 234,507 | 1,088,695 | −854,188 | 21.5% |
| 2021 | 69,806 | 829,314 | −759,508 | 8.4% |
| 2022 | 11,205 | 418,321 | −407,116 | 2.7% |
| 2023 | 4,037 | 389,265 | −385,228 | 1.0% |
| 2024 | 40,386 | 249,930 | −209,544 | **16.2%** |

**FIT ran an operating loss in every one of the six published years.** In 2024
operating costs were **6.2× freight revenue**. This is *before* any capital
charge, depreciation of new works, or debt service.

**Caveat, stated because it cuts the other way:** FIT's network was under
reconstruction through 2021–2023 (Línea Z works), which suppressed revenue —
the 2022 and 2023 figures are not steady-state. But **2019, before those works,
still shows only 36.2% cost recovery.** There is no year in the published record
in which this operator covered its operating costs.

## Ferrosur — the comparator the screen actually used

2024 operating margin **43.19%**, which reproduces ARTF's own *utilidad de
operación* of 3,688,169 to within rounding — an independent check that the
margin derivation in `margin-derivation.md` is right.

## What this does to the verdict

The supportable-capital analysis used **Ferrosur's** margin: +0.402 MXN per
net ton-km. That was already flagged as generous, because Ferrosur is a dense,
profitable, privately-run network. This quantifies *how* generous:

> The operator that would actually run this corridor recovers **16% of its
> operating costs** from freight revenue, and has never exceeded **36%**.

On FIT's realised economics the contribution margin available to service
rehabilitation capital is **negative**. There is no positive capital ceiling to
compute: the line would not cover the cost of running trains on it, before a
single peso of rehabilitation is repaid.

This does not replace the Ferrosur-based analysis — a reactivated corridor
might be run on a different cost base, and FIT's figures are distorted by the
reconstruction years. It bounds it. The screen's headline result (fails against
Mexican precedent capital cost even at 100% capture) used the **favourable**
end of the operator range. The unfavourable end is not a smaller surplus; it is
no surplus at all.

## Consequence for Step 6

This is precisely the question the public-benefit case exists to answer. If the
commercial case is negative under both operator assumptions, the remaining
question is whether external benefits — reduced road operating cost, pavement
wear avoided, accident and CO₂ reduction, consumer price effects in the Cañada
and Sierra Norte — justify a permanent operating subsidy, not merely a capital
grant. That is a different and legitimate question. **It is untested here.**
